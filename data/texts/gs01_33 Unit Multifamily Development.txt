Marcus & Millichap is pleased to present this offering for the sale of the development of 33 units located at 9850 E. Girard Avenue, Denver, CO 
80231. The develpoment consists of 34,076 SF of land, fully entitled and ready to develop 33 units of multifamily, located right off of Hampden 
Avenue and Havana Street in Denver, CO. The property has been fully vetted and entitled for the proposed develpoment. The proximity to the 
Denver Tech Center, the University of Denver and Cherry Creek, with quick access to both I-25 and I-225, make this a fantastic location for work-
force, student or young professional housing.  
 
The proposed development would be 33 units (24 two bed/two bath, 3 two bed/1.5 bath, 6 one bed/one bath). All units are proposed to have 
their own washer/dryer hookups, a patio/balcony, 9-10 foot ceilings and covered parking for some. The building would be fully enclosed and 
have an elevator, an amenity that is unique to the area. The proposed development would be the first new development in the area in over 25 
years.

DENVER, COLORADO MARKET OVERVIEW
Denver is at the center of Colorado’s Front Range, nestled at the conver-
gence of the Great Plains and the majestic Rocky Mountains. Most of the 
metro is situated east of the mountains. The Rocky Mountains act as a 
formidable barrier, protecting the metro from many strong Pacific winter 
storms. The eastern and northern reaches of the metro are expected to 
receive the majority of future development, as land in these areas is rela-
tively flat and affordable. Denver’s elevation of 5,280 feet above sea level 
provides it with the nickname: “Mile-High City.”
The Denver-Aurora-Broomfield metro consists of Broomfield, Arapahoe, 
Denver, Adams, Douglas and Jefferson counties. Denver, which is both a 
county and a city, is the largest of each, with more than 669,800 residents. 
The entire metro is expected to post an average population gain of 6.5 per-
cent during the next five years.
Denver’s economy is expanding, with the annual change in the metro’s gross 
metropolitan product (GMP) expected to reach 3.8 percent this year. Retail 
sales rose an estimated 4.4 percent last year, above the national average 
growth. The area benefits from its position as a regional business and govern-
ment hub. The state capitol is located in Denver, and the metro is home to ten 
Fortune 500 firms.
Key drivers of the region’s economy include aerospace, bioscience, energy, fi-
nancial services, healthcare, aviation, information technology and software 
development, and telecommunications. There are more than 400 aerospace 
companies and suppliers in the metro. Defense-related manufacturers such as 
Lockheed Martin and Raytheon also have significant operations in the metro. 
Supporting these sectors is a highly educated workforce and a base of compa-
nies that serve as a magnet for startups, as Denver is often recognized as fertile 
ground for entrepreneurs.
Denver also draws alternative energy companies such as Ascent Solar Technolo-
gies and Vestas Wind Systems, both of which have local headquarters. The National 
Renewable Energy Laboratory in Golden, the Department of Energy’s laboratory 
for renewable energy and energy-efficiency research and development, helps lure 
energy-related businesses.

MAJOR AREA EMPLOYERS & WORK FORCE 
Employers provide nearly 1.4 million jobs in the Denver metro. The area’s importance as a transportation and distribution hub 
is illustrated by the sector’s dominance. The trade, transportation and utilities segment employs nearly 252,000 workers, or 
18 percent of the workforce. Other major sectors include those serving the population, such as professional and business ser-
vices, which accounts for another 17 percent; government, with 14 percent; and education and health services, comprising 
13 percent of the local area jobs. Many healthcare companies are also significant employers.  
The metro’s vast concentration of white-collar employment has a significant effect on the economy, as these positions are 
generally higher-paying. On the other hand, the manufacturing workforce is relatively small, accounting for 5 percent of all 
jobs, well below the national average. 
During the next five-year period, all but one of the major employment sectors will register gains. The fastest-growing sectors 
will be construction, with a 3.6 percent gain; education and health services, with 3.0 percent; and leisure and hospitality with 
2.9 percent annual growth through 2020.
